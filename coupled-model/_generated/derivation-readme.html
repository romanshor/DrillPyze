Derivation of the DrillPyze coupled axial–torsional model

This document derives the model implemented in DrillPyze (kernel: src/DrillPyze/kernel.py), from the governing equations to the discrete scheme.

  • The torsional model follows Aarsnes & Shor (2018) and the control papers of Aarsnes, di Meglio & Shor (2018) and Auriol et al. (2022).

  • The axial model uses an identical formulation, with Young’s modulus in place of the shear modulus.

  • The two directions are cross-coupled in two places:

    1. along the string, through a single 2-D Coulomb friction acting on the combined axial and rotational sliding velocity, with a normal force that follows the live tension (§5);

    2. at the drill bit, through a Coulomb-type bit–rock interaction (§6.3).

  • A decoupled variant (along_string_coupling=False), in which the only coupling is at the bit, is kept for comparison (§5.4).

All quantities are SI. Notation is collected in §11.

1. Assumptions

  1. The drillstring is a slender, linearly elastic rod of length \(L\), with curvilinear coordinate \(x \in [0, L]\) measured from the top drive (\(x=0\)) to the bit (\(x=L\)).

  2. Only axial and torsional motion is modelled; lateral (bending/whirl) dynamics are not. The string lies on the low or high side of the borehole as determined by the soft-string normal force (§5.1).

  3. Material properties are piecewise constant over sections (drill pipe, HWDP, collars, …). Tool-joint mass is included through an effective density \(\rho = m/A\), where \(m\) is the mass per unit length and \(A\) the body area.

  4. Small strains, so axial and torsional wave speeds are independent of load.

  5. Drilling fluid enters through buoyancy (weight), linear viscous damping (steady Couette drag plus an oscillatory Stokes boundary layer) and, with the pumps off, a Bingham yield-stress drag (§5.6). Steel damping enters through a Rayleigh / Kelvin–Voigt term (§5.6). Hydraulics do not otherwise enter the mechanics.

  6. Contact with the borehole is Coulomb friction with distinct static and kinetic coefficients, separately for cased and open hole.

  7. Frequency range. The damping models are fitted at the first mode and at \(f_\max = 10\) Hz. Together with the numerical dissipation of the first-order scheme, this limits the model to vibrations below 10 Hz (§5.6.6). High-frequency torsional oscillations (HFTO, 50–500 Hz) and bit bounce harmonics are out of scope.

2. Kinematics and constitutive relations

2.1 Torsion

Let \(\phi(t,x)\) be the angular displacement and \(\omega = \partial\phi/\partial t\) the angular velocity. Following Aarsnes & Shor (2018), the torque transmitted downward through a cross-section is proportional to the twist per unit length:

\[\tau(t,x) = J G \,\frac{\phi(t,x) - \phi(t,x+\mathrm{d}x)}{\mathrm{d}x} = -JG\,\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x},\]

where \(J = \tfrac{\pi}{32}(D_o^4 - D_i^4)\) is the polar moment of area and \(G\) the shear modulus. Differentiating in time gives the compatibility equation

\[\frac{\partial \tau}{\partial t} + JG\,\frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x} = 0. \tag{2.1}\]

2.2 Axial (identical structure, \(G \to E\), \(J \to A\))

Let \(u(t,x)\) be the axial displacement (positive downhole) and \(v = \partial u/\partial t\) the axial velocity. The axial force transmitted downward is the compressive force

\[q(t,x) = -EA\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial x},\]

with \(A = \tfrac{\pi}{4}(D_o^2 - D_i^2)\), so \(q > 0\) is compression and the effective tension is \(T = -q\). Differentiating in time:

\[\frac{\partial q}{\partial t} + EA\,\frac{\partial v}{\partial x} = 0. \tag{2.2}\]

Choosing \(q\) (compression) rather than \(T\) makes the axial equations term for term identical to the torsional ones under \((\omega, \tau, J, G) \to (v, q, A, E)\).

3. Balance of momentum

Consider an element \([x, x + \mathrm{d}x]\).

3.1 Torsion

The rotational inertia of the element is \(\rho J\,\mathrm{d}x\). The net transmitted torque is \(\tau(x) - \tau(x+\mathrm{d}x)\), and the distributed torques are viscous damping and Coulomb wall friction:

\[\rho J\,\frac{\partial \omega}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial \tau}{\partial x} = -k_t\,\rho J\,\omega - \mathcal{F}_t(t,x), \tag{3.1}\]

where \(k_t\) (1/s) stands for the linear damping operators of §5.6 (fluid and material; in the original model a single constant rate), and \(\mathcal{F}_t\) is the frictional torque per unit length (an inclusion, §5).

3.2 Axial

The mass of the element is \(\rho A\,\mathrm{d}x\). In addition to the transmitted force, the element carries its buoyed weight per length \(w_b\) projected on the pipe axis, \(w_b \cos\theta\), where \(\theta(x)\) is the inclination:

\[\rho A\,\frac{\partial v}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial q}{\partial x} = w_b\cos\theta - k_a\,\rho A\, v - \mathcal{F}_a(t,x), \tag{3.2}\]

with

\[w_b = m\,g\left(1 - \frac{\rho_\text{mud}}{\rho_\text{steel}}\right),\]

\(k_a\) the axial damping rate, and \(\mathcal{F}_a\) the axial friction force per unit length.

3.3 Wave equations

Eliminating \(\tau\) from (2.1) and (3.1) in a uniform section without sources gives \(\partial_t^2\phi = c_t^2\,\partial_x^2\phi\). Likewise, (2.2) and (3.2) give \(\partial_t^2 u = c_a^2\,\partial_x^2 u\). The wave speeds are

\[c_t = \sqrt{G/\rho}, \qquad c_a = \sqrt{E/\rho}.\]

For steel, \(c_t \approx 3{,}180\) m/s and \(c_a \approx 5{,}140\) m/s.

4. Characteristic form

4.1 Hyperbolic system

Write the torsional system as \(U_t + \mathbf{A}\,U_x = S\) with \(U = (\tau, \omega)^\top\):

\[\begin{split}\mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & JG \\ 1/(\rho J) & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad S = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ S_t/(\rho J) \end{pmatrix}, \qquad S_t = -k_t\rho J\omega - \mathcal{F}_t .\end{split}\]

The eigenvalues are \(\pm\sqrt{G/\rho} = \pm c_t\).

4.2 Riemann invariants and impedance

Define the characteristic impedance

\[Z_t = \frac{JG}{c_t} = \rho J\,c_t = J\sqrt{G\rho} \qquad\text{(axial: } Z_a = A\sqrt{E\rho}\text{)},\]

and the Riemann invariants (Aarsnes & Shor 2018, eq. 11)

\[\alpha = \omega + \frac{\tau}{Z_t}, \qquad \beta = \omega - \frac{\tau}{Z_t}. \tag{4.1}\]

Using (2.1), (3.1) and \(JG/Z_t = c_t\), \(1/(\rho J) = c_t/Z_t\):

\[\partial_t\alpha = \partial_t\omega + \tfrac{1}{Z_t}\partial_t\tau = \frac{S_t - \partial_x\tau}{\rho J} - \frac{JG}{Z_t}\partial_x\omega = -c_t\,\partial_x\!\left(\omega + \tfrac{\tau}{Z_t}\right) + \frac{S_t}{\rho J},\]

so

\[\frac{\partial \alpha}{\partial t} + c_t\,\frac{\partial \alpha}{\partial x} = \frac{S_t}{\rho J}, \qquad \frac{\partial \beta}{\partial t} - c_t\,\frac{\partial \beta}{\partial x} = \frac{S_t}{\rho J}. \tag{4.2}\]

\(\alpha\) is a wave travelling down the string and \(\beta\) a wave travelling up. The physical variables are recovered as \(\omega = (\alpha+\beta)/2\) and \(\tau = Z_t(\alpha-\beta)/2\).

The axial invariants are \(\alpha_a = v + q/Z_a\), \(\beta_a = v - q/Z_a\), which travel at \(\pm c_a\) with source \(S_a/(\rho A)\).

4.3 Junctions between sections

At a change of section at \(x_i\) (e.g. pipe to collar), continuity requires \(\omega^- = \omega^+\) and \(\tau^- = \tau^+\). Given the incoming invariants, \(\alpha^-\) from above and \(\beta^+\) from below, these two conditions give the interface state

\[\tau^\ast = \frac{\alpha^- - \beta^+}{1/Z^- + 1/Z^+}, \qquad \omega^\ast = \alpha^- - \frac{\tau^\ast}{Z^-}. \tag{4.3}\]

The outgoing waves then follow from (4.1). For \(\bar Z = Z^+/Z^-\) (for one material, \(\bar Z = J_c/J_p\)), this reproduces the transmission and reflection relations of Aarsnes & Shor (2018), eqs. 16–17. Equation (4.3) is also the exact Riemann solution used at every cell face in the numerical scheme (§8).

4.4 Gravity: well-balanced split

The weight term in (3.2) is time-invariant. Write \(q = q_s(x) + q_d(t,x)\), where \(q_s\) is the compression of the frictionless string hanging freely with the bit off bottom:

\[\frac{\mathrm{d}q_s}{\mathrm{d}x} = w_b\cos\theta, \qquad q_s(L) = 0 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; q_s(x) = -\int_x^L w_b\cos\theta\,\mathrm{d}\xi \;(\le 0,\ \text{i.e. tension}).\]

Then \(q_d\) satisfies (2.2) and (3.2) without the weight term. Only \(q_d\) is transported. This makes the discrete scheme well balanced: a hanging string at rest is an exact discrete equilibrium. Hookload and WOB are reconstructed as \(\text{HKLD} = -(q_s(0) + q_d(0))\) and \(\text{WOB} = q(L) = q_d(L)\).

5. Along-string friction

5.1 Normal force (soft string)

For a cable element with unit tangent \(\mathbf{t}(x)\) and effective tension \(T\), equilibrium normal to the pipe gives the contact force per unit length (Johancsik et al. 1984, in vector form):

\[F_N(x) = \left\| T\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t}}{\mathrm{d}x} + w_b\,\mathbf{z}_\perp \right\|, \qquad \mathbf{z}_\perp = \hat{\mathbf{z}} - (\hat{\mathbf{z}}\cdot\mathbf{t})\,\mathbf{t}, \tag{5.1}\]

where \(\hat{\mathbf z}\) points vertically down. The tangent is interpolated along the minimum-curvature arcs of the survey. In a 2-D build section, (5.1) reduces to \(|T\,\mathrm{d}\theta/\mathrm{d}x - w_b\sin\theta|\): tension pulls the pipe against the high side and gravity against the low side. In a straight inclined hole it reduces to \(w_b\sin\theta\), the normal force of Aarsnes & Shor (2018), eq. 6.

In the coupled model (default) the normal force is evaluated with the live tension \(T = -(q_s + q_d)\). Axial loading (pick-up, slack-off, WOB transfer) therefore changes the normal force, and through it the rotary friction torque.

In the decoupled variant (§5.4) it is evaluated with the tension of the static hanging string, \(T_s = -q_s\), so \(F_N = F_N(x)\) does not depend on the dynamic state. In both cases it is re-evaluated whenever the string has moved more than refresh_distance along the hole.

5.2 Cross-coupled Coulomb inclusion (default)

The pipe surface at \(x\) slides against the borehole with the velocity

\[\begin{split}\mathbf V = \begin{pmatrix} v \\ \omega\, r_c \end{pmatrix} \qquad\text{(axial, tangential)},\end{split}\]

where \(r_c\) is the contact radius (tool joint or stabiliser). Coulomb’s law for a contact with normal force \(F_N\) is a single friction force in the plane of sliding, \(\boldsymbol{\mathcal F} = (\mathcal F_a,\ \mathcal F_\theta)\), with

\[\begin{split}\boldsymbol{\mathcal F} \in \begin{cases} \left\{\,\mu_k F_N\,\dfrac{\mathbf V}{\|\mathbf V\|}\,\right\}, & \text{sliding},\\[8pt] \left\{\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal F} : \|\boldsymbol{\mathcal F}\| \le \mu_s F_N\,\right\}, & \text{sticking}. \end{cases} \tag{5.2}\end{split}\]

The regime is set by the normalised sliding speed: the contact is sticking when

\[\left(\frac{v}{v_c}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\omega}{\omega_c}\right)^2 \le 1,\]

and sliding otherwise. The axial friction force per length is \(\mathcal F_a\), and the frictional torque per length in (3.1) is

\[\mathcal F_t = r_c\,\mathcal F_\theta .\]

Two cross-coupling mechanisms follow from (5.2):

  • Velocity split. Kinetic friction has fixed magnitude \(\mu_k F_N\) but is shared between the two directions in proportion to the velocity components:

    \[\mathcal F_a = \mu_k F_N\,\frac{v}{\sqrt{v^2 + \omega^2 r_c^2}}, \qquad \mathcal F_t = \mu_k F_N\,r_c\,\frac{\omega r_c}{\sqrt{v^2 + \omega^2 r_c^2}} .\]

    Rotating while tripping therefore reduces axial drag. Conversely, axial motion reduces the rotary torque and can change the stick-slip behaviour.

  • Normal-force coupling. \(F_N\) depends on the live tension (5.1), so axial loads change the torque along the string. This is the effect that Aarsnes & Shor (2018, §5.5) observed as a phase shift once axial motion started.

A sticking contact also breaks out under combined load. The friction capacity \(\mu_s F_N\) is shared, so torque and axial pull together can free the string when neither alone would.

5.3 Special case: no axial motion

For \(v \equiv 0\), (5.2) reduces exactly to the torsional inclusion of Aarsnes & Shor (2018, eqs. 4–5), with limits \(\mathcal F_{c,s} = \mu_s F_N r_c\) and \(\mathcal F_{c,k} = \mu_k F_N r_c\) and \(f_\text{rat} = \mu_k/\mu_s\):

\[\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_t(\omega, x) \in \begin{cases} \{\,\mathcal{F}_{c,k}(x)\,\}, & \omega > \omega_c,\\[2pt] [-\mathcal{F}_{c,s}(x),\ \mathcal{F}_{c,s}(x)], & |\omega| \le \omega_c,\\[2pt] \{-\mathcal{F}_{c,k}(x)\}, & \omega < -\omega_c . \end{cases}\end{split}\]

The published off-bottom results (break-off torque, stick-slip amplitude and period, §9) therefore carry over unchanged.

5.4 Decoupled variant (along_string_coupling=False)

For comparison, the axial and torsional inclusions can be applied independently, each in the 1-D form above, with thresholds \(v_c\) and \(\omega_c\). The normal force is then taken from the static hanging string (§5.1). There is no velocity split and no normal-force coupling, so the only axial–torsional coupling is at the bit.

5.5 Axial static friction

The static and kinetic factors \(\mu_s > \mu_k\) were identified from torsional start-ups (Aarsnes & Shor 2018). Axial friction factors from pick-up and slack-off are kinetic values (Johancsik et al. 1984).

What goes wrong if the torsional contrast is also applied axially. Applying the full contrast axially, with the step law and \(v_c = 0.01\) m/s, gives sustained axial stick-slip at 0 rpm. In slack-off at 200 m/h on Well A:

  • the bit alternates between rest and 600 m/h;

  • the cycle is about 3 s;

  • hookload swings by 40 kN.

The mechanism is the classic one for velocity-weakening friction on a long, compliant string driven at constant speed: a step from \(\mu_s\) to \(\mu_k\) is infinitely steep weakening.

Default. Static friction now acts on an ellipse in force space:

\[\left(\frac{\mathcal F_a}{\mu_{s,a}F_N}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\mathcal F_\theta}{\mu_s F_N}\right)^2 \le 1, \qquad \mu_{s,a} = \mu_k + c_a(\mu_s - \mu_k),\]

with \(c_a\) = axial_static_contrast = 0 by default. A cell that breaks out slides with the ellipse radius in the direction of motion for that step, then with \(\mu_k\). The torsional behaviour, and the cross-coupling of §5.2, are unchanged. The full contrast (\(c_a = 1\)) and the exponential weakening law remain available for research, for example to study axial breakover after connections.

The ±10% start-up ringing that remains is the first axial mode (0.42 Hz), excited by the step change in block speed. It decays with the axial damping, which §5.6 now derives from the mud and the steel rather than setting by hand.

5.6 Distributed damping: fluid and material

The lumped rates \(k_t, k_a\) of (3.1)–(3.2) had no physical basis and damped every mode at the same rate. They are replaced by three mechanisms with parameters from the mud report and the string. The constant rates remain as optional calibration terms (DampingParameters.k_t, k_a, default 0).

Choice of mechanisms. Five candidates were considered:

  • steady laminar (Couette) annular shear;

  • the oscillatory Stokes boundary layer;

  • the yield stress of a Bingham or Herschel–Bulkley mud;

  • surge/swab piston flow;

  • material damping of steel and tool joints.

For 5” drill pipe in an 8½” hole with a 20 cP mud:

  • Couette drag is \(c_0 \approx 0.24\) N s/m², so \(k \approx 0.008\) /s.

  • Stokes layer at the 0.4 Hz first axial mode (\(\delta \approx 3.6\) mm) gives \(c_1 \approx 4\) N s/m², so \(k \approx 0.1\) /s and \(\zeta \approx 0.02\)–0.03.

  • Surge/swab through the DP annulus is \(\approx 0.06\) N s/m² per metre. It matters only across tight BHA annuli and is left to a future lumped BHA term.

  • Steel: the measured loss factor of a steel member is \(\eta \approx 0.014\), and jointed metal structures are higher (Orban 2011). This gives \(\zeta = \eta/2 \approx 0.005\) for the string.

The oscillatory fluid layer therefore dominates, as Moharrami et al. (2024) and Jansen & van den Steen (1995) assume with lumped coefficients. The confined-annulus added mass of Chen et al. (1976) and Dolfo et al. (2020) applies to lateral motion and is not used here.

5.6.1 F1 — fluid: steady Couette drag

Laminar flow between the pipe (radius \(r_o\)) and the hole or casing (radius \(R\)), with viscosity \(\mu\) (the Bingham plastic viscosity if given), gives drag per unit length

\[f_{a,0} = \frac{2\pi\mu}{\ln(R/r_o)}\, v, \qquad m_{t,0} = \frac{4\pi\mu\, r_o^2 R^2}{R^2 - r_o^2}\,\omega. \tag{5.6}\]

These act on the full velocity and are the only viscous loads in steady rotation or tripping, so they enter torque-and-drag directly.

5.6.2 F1 — fluid: oscillatory Stokes layer

A wall oscillating tangentially at frequency \(\omega_f\) in a viscous fluid drives a boundary layer of thickness \(\delta = \sqrt{2\nu/\omega_f}\), with \(\nu = \mu/\rho_f\) (Stokes’ second problem). The in-phase (dissipative) part of the wall shear stress is

\[\tau_w = \sqrt{\rho_f\mu\,\omega_f/2}\; u_w .\]

For \(\delta \ll r_i\) and \(\delta \ll R - r_o\) (both hold for \(f \gtrsim 0.1\) Hz), each wall acts as a flat plate. Summing the inner and outer walls of the pipe, and integrating the moment \(\tau_w r\) for torsion, gives the drag per unit length

\[f_{a,1} = \underbrace{\sqrt{\tfrac{\rho_f\mu\omega_a}{2}}\;2\pi(r_o + r_i)}_{c_{a,1}}\; \tilde v, \qquad m_{t,1} = \underbrace{\sqrt{\tfrac{\rho_f\mu\omega_t}{2}}\;2\pi(r_o^3 + r_i^3)}_{c_{t,1}}\; \tilde\omega . \tag{5.7}\]

The mud inside the pipe is not entrained, consistent with the steel-only mass \(\rho A\). The coefficient grows as \(\sqrt{\omega_f}\), which is a fractional (half-order) time derivative. It is evaluated once at a reference frequency: by default the first (quarter-wave) mode of the string held at the top,

\[f_{a,1} = \frac{1}{4\sum_j h_j/c_{a,j}}, \qquad f_{t,1} = \frac{1}{4\sum_j h_j/c_{t,j}} . \tag{5.8}\]

This is the mode that dominates stick-slip and the tripping transients.

\(\tilde v, \tilde\omega\) are the oscillatory parts of the velocities. A uniformly moving pipe has no Stokes layer, only (5.6). Each cell therefore carries a low-pass state

\[\dot{\bar v} = \omega_c(v - \bar v), \qquad \tilde v = v - \bar v, \qquad \omega_c = \phi\,\omega_{a,1},\]

with \(\phi\) = high_pass_fraction = 0.25. At the reference frequency the filter passes \(1/(1+\phi^2) = 94\%\) of the Stokes damping; at steady state it passes none.

5.6.3 F2 — fluid: yield-stress drag

A Bingham mud with yield point \(\tau_y\) resists the start of motion of a gelled column with a velocity-independent wall stress. Following the Herschel–Bulkley drag form \(F_d = A_d\tau_y + k_f A_d(V/\Delta)^n\) (Lyons & Plisga 2005, as used in the Swansea thesis), the yield part per unit length,

\[f_y = \tau_y\,\pi d_o, \tag{5.9}\]

is added to the Coulomb capacity of §5.2 for both directions: \(\mu F_N \to \mu F_N + f_y\), in the static ellipse and while sliding. It is applied with the sliding velocity at the contact radius, which is a simplification when \(r_c \ne r_o\). While the mud circulates, it is already yielded in the annulus, so by default (5.9) applies only with the pumps off (yield_drag='pumps_off'; also 'always', 'off').

5.6.4 M1 — material: Rayleigh / Kelvin–Voigt

For a linear viscoelastic (Kelvin–Voigt) rod, \(\sigma = E(\varepsilon + \beta\dot\varepsilon)\). The strain rate is \(\dot\varepsilon = \partial_x v\), which adds

\[\rho A\,\partial_t v \mathrel{+}= \partial_x\!\left(\beta EA\,\partial_x v\right), \qquad \rho J\,\partial_t \omega \mathrel{+}= \partial_x\!\left(\beta GJ\,\partial_x \omega\right) \tag{5.10}\]

to the momentum equations, with modal damping ratio \(\zeta_n = \beta\omega_n/2\). Steel and its connections dissipate a nearly frequency-independent fraction of energy per cycle (hysteretic damping, \(\zeta = \eta/2\)). That law is not causal in the time domain, so it is approximated by a Rayleigh pair, adding a mass-proportional rate \(\alpha\) that acts on \(\tilde v, \tilde\omega\) like the Stokes term:

\[\zeta(\omega) = \frac{\alpha}{2\omega} + \frac{\beta\omega}{2}, \qquad \alpha = \frac{\eta\,\omega_1\omega_2}{\omega_1+\omega_2}, \quad \beta = \frac{\eta}{\omega_1+\omega_2}, \tag{5.11}\]

which gives \(\zeta = \eta/2\) exactly at the first mode \(\omega_1\) and at \(\omega_2 = 2\pi f_\max\), and \(\zeta < \eta/2\) in between. Moharrami et al. (2024) fit Rayleigh damping over modes 1–10 in the same way. A Kelvin–Voigt term tuned only at the first mode (\(\beta = \eta/\omega_1\)) was rejected: its \(\zeta \propto f\) over-damps 10 Hz content by roughly 40×. The default is \(\eta = 0.01\).

5.6.5 Discretisation

Each step, after the Godunov flux update (§8.1):

  1. Linear fluid and \(\alpha\) terms, implicitly per cell:

    \[\omega^{**} = \frac{\omega^* + \Delta t\,k_{t,1}\bar\omega}{1 + \Delta t(k_{t,0} + k_{t,1})}, \qquad k_{t,0} = \frac{c_{t,0}}{\rho J} + k_t,\quad k_{t,1} = \frac{c_{t,1}}{\rho J} + \alpha_t,\]

    and likewise for \(v\).

  2. Kelvin–Voigt, implicitly, by one tridiagonal (Thomas) solve per direction:

    \[\frac{m_j h_j}{\Delta t}(x_j' - x_j) = \kappa_{j+\frac12}(x'_{j+1} - x'_j) - \kappa_{j-\frac12}(x'_j - x'_{j-1}), \qquad \kappa_{j+\frac12} = \beta\,\frac{\overline{EA}}{\bar h},\]

    with \(\kappa = 0\) at the top-drive and bit faces. An explicit step would be unstable: on a 10 m grid the diffusion limit \(h^2/(2\beta c^2)\) is far below the CFL step. The implicit step:

    • conserves momentum \(\sum m_j h_j x_j\);

    • dissipates \(\sum\kappa\,(\Delta x')^2 \ge 0\);

    • costs \(O(N)\).

  3. Friction (§8.3), with capacity \(\mu F_N + f_y\).

  4. Low-pass update: \(\bar x \leftarrow \bar x + (1 - e^{-\omega_c\Delta t})(x - \bar x)\).

The low-pass states are part of the simulation state, so pausing stays exact (§8.5).

5.6.6 Damping ratios, numerical dissipation and the 10 Hz limit

For a mode of frequency \(f\) (\(\omega = 2\pi f\)), the model gives

\[\zeta_\text{fluid} = \frac{k_0 + k_{1,S}H}{2\omega},\quad \zeta_\text{mat} = \frac{\alpha H}{2\omega} + \frac{\beta\omega}{2},\quad \zeta_\text{num} = \frac{\pi f h (1-\nu)}{c},\quad H = \frac{1}{1 + (\phi f_1/f)^2}, \tag{5.12}\]

where \(\zeta_\text{num}\) is the amplitude loss of first-order upwinding. Upwinding has modified-equation diffusion \(c h(1-\nu)/2\), with \(\nu\) the Courant number of that wave. Simulation.damping_summary(f) tabulates these per component.

For Well A drill pipe (\(f_{a,1}\) = 0.51 Hz, \(f_{t,1}\) = 0.32 Hz), the first modes have \(\zeta_\text{fluid}\) = 0.027 (axial) and 0.037 (torsional), with \(\zeta_\text{mat}\) = 0.0047. Previously the default \(k\) = 0.1 /s gave 0.016 and 0.025.

Limitations (stated validity: \(f \le 10\) Hz).

  • The Stokes coefficient is fixed at \(f_1\). Above it, fluid damping is under-predicted by \(\sqrt{f/f_1}\): 4–6× at 10 Hz, an absolute shortfall of \(\zeta \approx 0.005\).

  • Frequency-dependent Stokes damping would need a fractional-derivative (Prony / auxiliary-state) form; it is deferred.

  • Material damping follows (5.11) within the band and over-damps above \(f_\max\).

  • Numerical dissipation grows as \(f\,h\). With \(h\) = 10 m, the torsional \(\zeta_\text{num}\) reaches 0.04 at 10 Hz and exceeds the physical damping above about 2 Hz; the axial \(\zeta_\text{num}\) stays below 0.003. For torsional content up to 10 Hz, use \(h \lesssim 2.5\) m (\(\zeta_\text{num} \approx 0.01\)), at about 4× the run time.

  • Herschel–Bulkley shear thinning in the Stokes layer, which depends on amplitude, and the gel-strength build-up with time are not modelled.

6. Boundary conditions

At each boundary one invariant arrives from the interior. The boundary model supplies one relation, which closes the system.

6.1 Top drive (torsion)

At \(x = 0\) the incoming invariant is \(\beta_1\) (travelling up). The characteristic relation \(\beta = \omega - \tau/Z_t\) gives the pipe torque at the top drive:

\[\tau_0 = Z_t\,(\omega_{TD} - \beta_1). \tag{6.1}\]

The top drive is a rigid inertia \(I_{TD}\) driven by motor torque \(\tau_m\):

\[I_{TD}\,\dot\omega_{TD} = \tau_m - \tau_0 = \tau_m - Z_t\,(\omega_{TD} - \beta_1). \tag{6.2}\]

Seen from the string, (6.2) is a damper of impedance \(Z_t\) in parallel with the inertia. It is integrated implicitly in \(\omega_{TD}\), which is unconditionally stable:

\[\omega_{TD}^{k+1} = \frac{\dfrac{I_{TD}}{\Delta t}\,\omega_{TD}^{k} + \tau_m + Z_t\,\beta_1}{\dfrac{I_{TD}}{\Delta t} + Z_t}.\]

The motor torque comes from a controller (§7), from an external source, or is implied by a prescribed \(\omega_{TD}(t)\) via \(\tau_m = \tau_0 + I_{TD}\,\dot\omega_{TD}\).

6.2 Drawworks (axial)

At \(x = 0\) the block velocity \(v_\text{blk}\) (positive lowering) is prescribed. The incoming axial invariant \(\beta_{a,1}\) gives

\[q_d(0) = Z_a\,(v_\text{blk} - \beta_{a,1}), \qquad \text{HKLD} = -\bigl(q_s(0) + q_d(0)\bigr).\]

In autodriller mode, \(v_\text{blk}\) is the output of a PI controller acting on the surface WOB estimate \(\text{HKLD}_\text{off-bottom} - \text{HKLD}\).

The hook cannot push. If the prescribed block speed would make \(\text{HKLD} < 0\), the block stacks out. Hookload is then held at zero, so \(q_d(0) = -q_s(0)\), and the top of the string moves freely with \(v(0) = \beta_{a,1} + q_d(0)/Z_a\). For example, with the bit on bottom at 0 rpm, WOB levels off at the buoyed string weight less friction, instead of growing without limit.

Set-point ramps. Inputs without a controller approach a new value linearly over a short ramp time (ModelParameters.ramps). Defaults: the prescribed top-drive speed 1 s, the block velocity 2 s, the pump rate and the inlet temperature 3 s. The ramp is evaluated inside the time stepping from a table of (start value, start time, ramp time, target). Set points that feed a controller (the speed set point of §7, the autodriller WOB set point) are not ramped. See hydraulics.md §6.

Hydraulics. Standpipe pressure, bottomhole pressure and ECD come from hydraulics.md: chapter 7 steady losses, plus single-phase drift-flux transients after Wang et al. (2023).

An optional block-acceleration limit (DrawworksParameters.block_acceleration) rate-limits changes in \(v_\text{blk}\).

6.3 Drill bit: coupling at the bit

At \(x = L\) the incoming invariants are \(\alpha_t\) (torsion) and \(\alpha_a\) (axial). The characteristic relations are

\[\omega_b = \alpha_t - \frac{\text{TOB}}{Z_t}, \qquad v_b = \alpha_a - \frac{\text{WOB}}{Z_a}. \tag{6.3}\]

Both directions are closed by the bit–rock interaction law below. Because TOB depends on WOB and the penetration rate depends on \(\omega_b\), this law is the coupling between the two systems.

Off bottom (bit depth \(<\) hole depth): \(\text{WOB} = \text{TOB} = 0\), so \(\omega_b = \alpha_t\) and \(v_b = \alpha_a\). Each free end reflects its incident wave with the same sign.

Unilateral contact. On bottom, the bit can only push on the rock: \(\text{WOB} \ge 0\). If \(\alpha_a \le 0\) (the string is pulling the bit up), the bit lifts off: \(\text{WOB} = 0\) and \(v_b = \alpha_a\).

Rock strength. The drilling strength \(S\) is either a constant or, with strength_source='formation', \(S = \zeta\,\text{CCS}_\text{eff}/\eta\). In the second case \(S\) is computed from the formation, pore pressure, bottomhole pressure and temperatures at the bit. See rock-strength-and-temperature.md, which also derives the wellbore temperature model (Wang et al. 2023, SPE-214836-MS).

Quasi-static models (coulomb, cutting). Sticking applies when \(|\omega_b| \le \omega_c\): the bit stays at rest, and \(\text{TOB} = Z_t\alpha_t\) from (6.3), as long as that is within the sticking limit. When sliding, \(\text{TOB}\) is limited to \(|Z_t\alpha_t|\), so the bit is never driven backwards within a step. Combining the penetration law with (6.3): if \(Z_a\alpha_a \le W_0\) (with \(W_0 = W_f\), or 0 for Coulomb) or \(\omega_b = 0\), then \(v_b = 0\) and \(\text{WOB} = Z_a\alpha_a\). Otherwise

\[\text{WOB} = \frac{\alpha_a + k_r W_0 |\omega_b|}{1/Z_a + k_r|\omega_b|}, \qquad k_r = \frac{1}{2\pi a S}.\]

These are solved by fixed-point iteration on \(\omega_b\).

Regenerative model.

  • Profile. The hole bottom is stored as a profile \(s(\theta)\) over 720 angular bins.

  • Depth of cut. Blade \(j\), at angle \(\varphi_b + 2\pi j/n\), cuts to depth \(d_j = (u_b - s(\theta_j))^+\), measured against the surface left by the previous blade:

\[\text{WOB} = S a \sum_j d_j, \qquad \text{TOB} = \frac{a}{2\zeta}\,\text{WOB}.\]

  • Axial contact. The contact is solved implicitly for the new bit depth, \(u_b^{k+1} = u_b^k + \Delta t\,(\alpha_a - \text{WOB}/Z_a)\), by iterating on the set of blades in contact. If the bit rises above the profile, WOB is zero (lift-off).

  • Sweep. After each step the blades sweep from \(\varphi_b^k\) to \(\varphi_b^{k+1}\), and the bins they leave are set to the bit depth. The depth of cut therefore carries the delay of one blade passage. That delay is the regenerative effect, a coupling between axial and torsional motion that can self-excite.

  • Hole depth. The hole depth is the deepest point of the profile.

Coupling mechanisms at the bit. These act in addition to the along-string friction coupling of §5.2.

  • Torsion depends on axial: TOB is proportional to WOB in every model, so axial waves that change WOB modulate the torsional boundary and can trigger or suppress stick-slip.

  • Axial depends on torsion: penetration needs rotation. A stalled bit stops penetrating, WOB rises, and the sticking limit rises with it.

  • In the regenerative model the depth of cut also remembers the previous blade passage. That delay can destabilise steady drilling, producing bit bounce and stick-slip.

7. Top-drive controllers (Aarsnes, di Meglio & Shor 2018)

All three industrial controllers fit one block diagram. A set point \(\omega_{SP}\), a filtered measured speed \(\hat\omega_0\) (time constant \(t_{en}\)) and an optional impedance feedback of band-pass filtered pipe torque give

\[e = \omega_{SP} - \hat\omega_0 - Z\,\frac{s}{(s + 1/t_{hp})(1 + t_{lp}s)}\,\tau_0, \qquad \tau_m = K_p\,e + K_i\!\int e\,\mathrm{d}t,\]

8. Numerical scheme

8.1 Finite volumes with Godunov fluxes

The string is divided into cells \(j = 1..N\) of length \(h_j \le \Delta x\), aligned with section boundaries so each cell has uniform properties. Cell averages \((\omega_j, \tau_j)\) and \((v_j, q_{d,j})\) are stored.

At each face \(j+\tfrac12\) the exact Riemann solution (4.3) is evaluated from \(\alpha_j\) (cell above) and \(\beta_{j+1}\) (cell below). The boundary faces use §6. The conservative update is

\[\tau_j^{*} = \tau_j^k - \frac{\Delta t\,J_jG_j}{h_j}\left(\omega^\ast_{j+1/2} - \omega^\ast_{j-1/2}\right), \qquad \omega_j^{*} = \omega_j^k - \frac{\Delta t}{\rho_j J_j h_j}\left(\tau^\ast_{j+1/2} - \tau^\ast_{j-1/2}\right),\]

and identically for \((q_d, v)\) with \((EA, \rho A)\).

Inside a uniform section this is exactly the first-order upwind scheme on the Riemann invariants (Aarsnes & Shor 2018, eqs. 23–24). At junctions it enforces (4.3) without special cases. First-order upwinding is used deliberately: the set-valued friction introduces temporal discontinuities that make higher-order schemes oscillate.

8.2 Time step

Stability requires \(\Delta t \le h_j / c\) for both waves. The axial wave is faster, so

\[\Delta t = \text{CFL}\cdot\min_j \frac{h_j}{c_{a,j}}, \qquad \text{CFL} = 0.95,\]

and the torsional Courant number is \(\text{CFL}\,c_t/c_a \approx 0.59\). At this Courant number upwinding is slightly diffusive, but the tests show negligible amplitude loss for the low modes that dominate stick-slip (§10).

8.3 Source terms (operator splitting)

After the flux update, each cell applies its sources:

  1. Damping, implicitly: the fluid and Rayleigh-\(\alpha\) rates per cell, then the Kelvin–Voigt tridiagonal solve (§5.6.5). With only the calibration rates this reduces to \(\omega^{**} = \omega^*/(1 + k_t\Delta t)\).

  2. Friction, implicitly (coupled model). Let \(\mathbf V^{*} = (v^{**},\ \omega^{**} r_c)\) be the predicted sliding velocity and \(M = \operatorname{diag}(m_a, m_\theta)\) the mass per length in each direction, with \(m_a = \rho A\) and \(m_\theta = \rho J / r_c^2\). Coulomb friction (5.2) is applied with a backward-Euler step:

    \[\mathbf V^{k+1} = \mathbf V^{*} - \Delta t\, M^{-1}\,\mu F_N\,\frac{\mathbf V^{k+1}}{\|\mathbf V^{k+1}\|}, \qquad c \equiv \Delta t\,\mu F_N,\]

    with \(\mu = \mu_s\) if the cell was sticking at step \(k\) and \(\mu = \mu_k\) otherwise. Two cases follow:

    • Stick. If \(\|M\mathbf V^{*}\| \le c\), the friction impulse can absorb the cell’s momentum, and \(\mathbf V^{k+1} = 0\) exactly. The impulse needed to stop the cell, \(M\mathbf V^*/\Delta t\), is the 2-D form of \(\tilde{\mathcal F}\) in Aarsnes & Shor (2018, eqs. 20–21).

    • Slide. Otherwise each component satisfies

      \[V_i^{k+1} = \frac{m_i V_i^{*}\, s}{m_i s + c}, \qquad g(s) = \sum_i \left(\frac{m_i V_i^{*}}{m_i s + c}\right)^2 - 1 = 0,\]

      where \(s = \|\mathbf V^{k+1}\|\). The function \(g\) is convex and decreasing, with \(g(0) > 0 \ge g(\|\mathbf V^*\|)\), so Newton’s method from \(s = \|\mathbf V^*\|\) converges monotonically after the first step. For equal masses this reduces to \(s = \|\mathbf V^*\| - c/m\).

    The implicit form guarantees three properties:

    • friction opposes the new sliding direction exactly;

    • friction never reverses the motion within a step;

    • sticking is exact.

    An explicit update with each component clipped separately would distort the friction direction when one component is small.

    Decoupled variant. Each direction is projected separately:

    \[\mathcal F = \operatorname{sign}(\tilde{\mathcal F})\,\min\!\left(|\tilde{\mathcal F}|,\ \mathcal F_c\right), \qquad \tilde{\mathcal F}_t = \rho J\,\omega^{**}/\Delta t, \qquad \tilde{\mathcal F}_a = \rho A\,v^{**}/\Delta t,\]

    which is the published scheme (eq. 22). For \(v \equiv 0\) the coupled update reduces to exactly this.

8.4 Moving string

The grid is attached to the pipe. As the bit advances by \(s\), cell \(j\) moves to \(x_j + s\). Every refresh_distance of travel, the cell geometry, the cased/open friction zones, \(q_s\) and \(F_N\) are re-evaluated. Total compression is kept continuous across a refresh by setting \(q_d \leftarrow q_d + q_s^\text{old} - q_s^\text{new}\).

8.5 Pausing

The state consists of the cell arrays, the top-drive, controller and filter states, and the bit and hole depths. All of it persists between calls, and inputs are held constant within a call. A sequence of short step calls is therefore bit-identical to one long call; the steps are counted in integers against the accumulated requested time.

9. Steady-state limits and analytic results

These limits connect the model to classical results and are used in the tests.

  • Torque and drag. Setting \(\partial_t = 0\) in (3.1)–(3.2) with kinetic friction (5.2) gives the soft-string torque and drag equations, integrated from the bit up:

    \[\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{\mathrm{d}x} = \mathcal F_a - w_b\cos\theta, \qquad \frac{\mathrm{d}\tau}{\mathrm{d}x} = -\mathcal F_t, \qquad T(L) = -\text{WOB},\ \tau(L) = \text{TOB}.\]

    This is statics.torque_drag, with friction split by the velocity direction exactly as in (5.2). The coupled dynamic model converges to it in steady combined motion. The decoupled variant is compared with it using the static normal force (normal_force=sim.fn0).

  • Break-off torque (bit off bottom; Aarsnes & Shor 2018, eqs. 26–27):

    \[\tau_\text{max}(x) = \int_x^L \mathcal F_{c,s}\,\mathrm{d}\xi .\]

  • Stick-slip amplitude and period (Aarsnes & Shor 2018, eqs. 39–40):

    \[\Delta\tau_m \approx 2\,\tau_\text{max}(0)\,(1 - f_\text{rat}), \qquad t_p \approx \frac{\Delta\phi(0)}{\omega_{TD}} + t_{fp}, \qquad \Delta\phi(0) = 2(1-f_\text{rat})\int_0^L\frac{\tau_\text{max}}{JG}\,\mathrm{d}x .\]

  • Free vibration of a uniform string with a fixed top and free bit: wave arrival at the bit after \(L/c\), and fundamental frequency \(f_1 = c/(4L)\).

  • Bit.

    • Coulomb: breakout at \(\mu_s\,\text{WOB}\,D/3\); while sliding, \(\text{TOB}/\text{WOB} = \mu_k D/3\).

    • Cutting: steady drilling at block speed \(V\) and bit speed \(\Omega\) gives \(\text{WOB} = W_f + 2\pi a S V/\Omega\) and \(\text{TOB} = a\,\text{WOB}/(2\zeta)\).

    • Regenerative: the same steady state as cutting with \(W_f = 0\) when it is stable.
