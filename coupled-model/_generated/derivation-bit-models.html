Bit–rock interaction: from the Bit Model Library to three reduced models

This note analyses the four PDC bit models in the Bit Model Library (SPE-223715-MS) and reduces them to three models, each with two or three parameters, for use as the bit boundary condition in DrillPyze.

The models in the library are:

  • Ziaja (1985);

  • Detournay & Defourny (1992);

  • Gerbaud et al. (2006);

  • Che et al. (2016).

The script bit_library_analysis.py in this folder reproduces every number below from the library’s code and sample data.

Notation:

  • \(a = D/2\) is the bit radius;

  • \(\omega\) is the bit speed;

  • \(v\) is the axial bit velocity;

  • \(d = 2\pi v/\omega\) is the depth of cut per revolution;

  • \(W\) is weight on bit (WOB) and \(T\) is torque on bit (TOB).

1. What each library model is

Detournay & Defourny (1992)

The bit force is split into a cutting part and a frictional-contact part (wear flats):

\[W = \underbrace{\zeta\varepsilon a d}_{W_c} + \underbrace{a\ell\sigma}_{W_f}, \qquad T = \underbrace{\tfrac12\varepsilon a^2 d}_{T_c} + \underbrace{\tfrac12\mu\gamma a W_f}_{T_f}.\]

  • Inputs: \(\varepsilon\) (intrinsic specific energy), \(\zeta\), \(\ell\) (wear-flat length), \(\sigma\) (contact pressure), \(\mu\), \(\gamma\) (bit constant) and \(a\). That is six rock and bit parameters plus geometry.

  • What the output depends on: in the library’s ROP calculation, the result depends only on the product \(\zeta\varepsilon\) and on \(W_f\). The elasticities are −0.91 to \(\varepsilon\), −0.91 to \(\zeta\) and −0.14 to each of \(\sigma\) and \(\ell\); \(\mu\) has no effect.

Ziaja (1985)

This is a cutter-level model.

  • Forces per cutter: the cutting forces on each cutter are \(R_c S_c\), where \(S_c(d)\) is the chord area of a round cutter. They are split by the back rake angle into a normal part, \(R_c S_c\sin\alpha\), and a tangential part, \(R_c S_c\cos\alpha\). A wear-flat force \(R_p S_w\) is added.

  • Inputs: \(R_c\), \(R_p\), \(\alpha\), \(\beta\), \(d_c\), \(n_c\), wear length and \(\mu\).

  • Normal-to-tangential ratio: it is fixed at \(\tan\alpha\), which is 0.21 in the sample.

  • ROP: the library computes ROP as \(\text{rpm}\cdot d\cdot n_c\), with \(d\) as an input. So ROP has no dependence on WOB (elasticity 0) and is proportional to \(d\) and \(n_c\).

Che et al. (2016)

This is a single-cutter chipping and crushing model.

  • Normal force: \(F_n = \sigma_c\,w\,g(\alpha, \sigma_t/\sigma_c)\,d\), which is linear in depth of cut. In the sample, \(g = 1.57\).

  • Use in the library: it is scaled to the bit with \(n_c\), after subtracting the Detournay–Defourny wear-flat force.

  • What ROP depends on: WOB (elasticity 1.14), \(\sigma_c\) (−0.9), cutter width (−0.91) and \(W_f\) (−0.14). \(\sigma_t\) and \(\alpha\) hardly matter (≤ 0.08).

  • Torque: the model gives none.

Gerbaud et al. (2006)

This is a cutter model with cutting, chamfer and wear-flat terms.

  • Cutting force: the normal cutting force is \(\tan(\theta_f + \alpha)\,A\,\varepsilon\). So Gerbaud’s effective normal-to-tangential ratio is \(\tan(\theta_f+\alpha)\), which is 1.80 in the sample.

  • ROP: in the library ROP follows the cut area \(A\) (elasticity 0.93) and \(\theta_f\) (2.2), and has no dependence on WOB, because \(A\) is supplied as an input.

2. What they have in common

With the bookkeeping removed, all four models share one structure:

\[\boxed{\;W = W_f + S\,a\,d, \qquad T = \frac{a}{2\zeta}\,W_c + \mu_f\,\frac{a}{2}\,W_f\;}\]

  1. Cutting forces are proportional to the depth of cut. This is exact in Detournay & Defourny, Che and Gerbaud. Ziaja’s chord area is nominally nonlinear, but see §5.

    • Every rock and cutter input (\(\sigma_c\), \(\sigma_t\), \(R_c\), \(\alpha\), \(\beta\), \(\theta_f\), \(d_c\), \(w\), \(n_c\), \(A\)) enters only through two numbers. The first is the drilling strength \(S = \zeta\varepsilon\), the axial cutting force per unit cut area. The second is the force ratio \(\zeta\), normal over tangential cutting force.

    • Che and Gerbaud are best read as ways to estimate \(S\) (and \(\zeta\)) from rock strength and cutter geometry, not as different laws.

  2. Wear-flat or chamfer contact adds a force that does not depend on depth of cut. This is \(W_f\), with its frictional torque. It appears as \(a\ell\sigma\) in Detournay & Defourny, \(R_p S_w\) in Ziaja and \(\sigma_0 A_f\) in Gerbaud, and Che subtracts it.

  3. All four are rate independent. None of them has velocity weakening. Torque depends on \(d\) and \(W\), not on \(\omega\). For drillstring dynamics this matters: at the bit, stick-slip then has to come from somewhere other than these laws. The options are a static/kinetic friction difference, the regenerative (delayed) depth of cut, or along-string friction.

Three things identify the bit from field data:

  • the slope of TOB against WOB, which gives \(a/(2\zeta)\);

  • the slope of WOB against \(d = 2\pi\,\text{ROP}/\omega\), which gives \(Sa\);

  • that line’s intercept, which gives \(W_f\).

These are the three numbers the reduced models keep.

3. Simplifications and why

Simplification

Justification

Effect in the sample data

Collapse every rock and cutter input into \(S\) and \(\zeta\)

Every model is linear in \(d\); the inputs only scale the slope (§2)

Exact

Contact friction \(\mu_f = \mu\gamma \approx 1/\zeta\), so \(T = aW/(2\zeta)\) over the whole range

Removes a parameter that cannot be told apart from \(\zeta\) without data at very low WOB

Sample has \(\mu\gamma = 0.9\) and \(1/\zeta = 1.0\), so the torque error is \(\tfrac a2 W_f(1/\zeta-\mu\gamma) \approx\) 1.7% of TOB

Linear rather than chord-area dependence on \(d\)

Over 0.0005–0.01 in, the Ziaja area as implemented scales as \(d^{-0.02}\), so it is not usable as a nonlinearity. For a correct chord area the nonlinearity matters only at depths of cut well below field values

–

Depth of cut per bit revolution, \(d = 2\pi v/\omega\), not per cutter × \(n_c\)

Avoids double counting cutters (§5) and needs no cutter layout

–

Keep a static/kinetic difference only in the friction-dominated model

Field evidence of bit-induced velocity weakening is for worn bits in the contact regime

4. The three reduced models

All three are implemented in the kernel (BitParameters.model), selectable in the UI, and share the drilling strength \(S\).

Model 1: Coulomb (friction-dominated)

Parameters: \(\mu_s\), \(\mu_k\), \(S\).

\[|T| \le \mu_s\,W\,\tfrac{D}{3}\ \ (\text{sticking}),\qquad T = \mu_k\,W\,\tfrac{D}{3}\ \ (\text{sliding}),\qquad \text{ROP} = \frac{\omega\,W}{2\pi a S}.\]

  • Physics: this is the Detournay & Defourny model in the contact limit (\(W \approx W_f\), a worn or dull bit), where torque is friction, \(T = \mu\gamma\tfrac a2 W\). To that it adds the static/kinetic difference that gives velocity weakening and bit-induced stick-slip.

  • Fitting:

    • \(\mu_k\) from TOB/WOB while drilling;

    • \(\mu_s\) from the breakout torque on bottom;

    • \(S\) from the slope of ROP/RPM against WOB.

Model 2: Cutting (Detournay & Defourny, quasi-static)

Parameters: \(S\), \(\zeta\), \(W_f\).

\[W = W_f + S\,a\,d,\qquad T = \frac{a}{2\zeta}\,W,\qquad \text{ROP} = \frac{\omega\,(W-W_f)^+}{2\pi a S}.\]

  • Physics: a sharp to moderately worn PDC bit with a cutting response. It has a WOB threshold before penetration and no velocity weakening. The bit sticks only when the string cannot supply \(aW/(2\zeta)\).

  • Fitting:

    • \(\zeta\) from the slope of TOB against WOB;

    • \(S\) and \(W_f\) from a straight-line fit of WOB against \(d = 2\pi\,\text{ROP}/\omega\).

Model 3: Regenerative cutting (Richard, Germay & Detournay 2007)

Parameters: \(S\), \(\zeta\), plus the blade count \(n\) (bit geometry).

\[W = S\,a\sum_{j=1}^{n} d_j,\qquad T = \frac{a}{2\zeta}\,W,\qquad d_j = \bigl(u_b - s(\theta_j)\bigr)^+ .\]

  • Physics: the same cutting law, but the depth of cut of each blade is measured against the hole-bottom profile \(s(\theta)\) left by the previous blade. The profile is stored over 720 angular bins and updated as the blades sweep. This brings in the one-blade-passage delay (the regenerative effect), which couples axial and torsional motion at the bit.

  • Behaviour in the model:

    • Lift-off and stalling come out naturally: if the bit rises above the profile, \(W = 0\); if it stops, the profile stops being cut.

    • With enough damping it settles to exactly the Model 2 state with \(W_f = 0\) (test test_regenerative_bit_converges_to_cutting_when_stable).

    • With light damping it self-excites into coupled bit bounce and stick-slip (test test_regenerative_bit_self_excites_when_lightly_damped).

  • Fitting: the same \(S\) and \(\zeta\) as Model 2. The steady states agree, so no extra fitting is needed. \(n\) comes from the bit specification.

Rock strength

In all three models, \(S\) can be fixed or computed from the formation at the bit. That calculation covers confinement, pore pressure, temperature and mud cooling; see rock-strength-and-temperature.md.

Choosing between them

Situation

Model

Worn or dull bit; torque dominated by friction; on-bottom stick-slip seen in the data

Coulomb

PDC bit at steady drilling; ROP and torque fitting; controller design for torque on bottom

Cutting

Studying bit bounce, coupled axial–torsional instability, or how blade count, ROP and RPM affect stability

Regenerative

5. Issues found in the library code and sample data

These came up while reducing the models. They don’t affect the reduced models above, which follow the published equations, but they explain some of the sensitivities in §1 and are worth checking in the library.

  1. Detournay & Defourny torque:

    • detournay_wob_tob omits the factor ½ on both \(T_c\) and \(T_f\), so the torque comes out twice the published value.

    • detournay_rop inverts the torque with the ½ included, so the two functions are inconsistent.

    • d_avg is computed but not used, so ROP comes from \(d_{WOB}\) only.

  2. Ziaja:

    • The chord-area expression for \(S_c(d)\) does not go to zero as \(d \to 0\) (0.19 in² at \(d = 0\)) and is almost independent of \(d\). This suggests a transcription error.

    • The wear-flat term is multiplied by \(n_c\) twice: 4,970 lbf instead of 108 lbf in the sample.

  3. Depth of cut in the cutter models: Ziaja, Che and Gerbaud use ROP = rpm·\(d\)·\(n_c\). This treats every cutter as removing a full layer each revolution. In Che, \(n_c\) cancels because force is also divided by \(n_c\). In Gerbaud it does not, so ROP scales roughly as \(n_c^2\) (elasticity 2.1).

  4. Gerbaud: the cut area \(A\) is an input rather than being computed from the depth of cut, so the calculation is circular, and ROP does not respond to WOB.

  5. Sample data units: with the tob column read as lbf·ft, the implied bit friction \(2T/(aW)\) is 8.5, which is physically implausible. Read as lbf·in it is 0.71, which is typical, so the column is probably lbf·in.
